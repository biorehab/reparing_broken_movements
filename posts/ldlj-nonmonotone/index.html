LDLJ: Non-Monotonic Behaviour with Inter-Submovement Interval

Companion simulation for the blog post on the LDLJ measure of movement smoothness

Each submovement is a 1-second minimum-jerk trajectory (MJT) of unit amplitude. The inter-submovement interval (ISI) is the onset-to-onset delay between consecutive submovements — ISI = 0 means all submovements start simultaneously (complete overlap), ISI = 1 s means they are perfectly end-to-end, and ISI > 1 s means there are gaps between them.

Both LDLJ curves are pre-computed across the full ISI range at startup. Drag the slider to inspect the speed profile and LDLJ value at any ISI.

What to observe

Note that although in general the curve tends to go up with decrease in the intersubmovement interval, there are multiple local regions where the monotonicity breaks down. The complex behavior is a complex interplay between the three different factors in the LDLJ formula: the total duration term ($T^3$) grows with ISI, the peak speed normalisation term ($v_p^2$) also changes with ISI as submovements combine or separate, and the ISJ term has multiple ISI-dependent transitions due to changes in the number of jerk oscillations within the movement duration.

Compare the 2-submovement and 6-submovement curves. With more submovements, there are more ISI-dependent transitions in jerk, and the normalisation term grows even faster — together, these amplify the non-monotonic oscillations.

Also, notice that this non-monotonic behaviour is more pronounced with the MJT profile than with the Gaussian profile. The exact share of submovements determines the detailed response of the measure to ISI changes.
