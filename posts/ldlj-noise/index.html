LDLJ: Effect of Measurement Noise

Companion simulation for the blog post on the LDLJ measure of movement smoothness

Real movement recordings always contain measurement noise. This simulation explores how additive Gaussian noise affects the LDLJ smoothness estimate — and how a low-pass filter can mitigate that effect.

A random movement consisting of up to 8 minimum jerk trajectory (MJT) submovements is generated on demand. The duration slider rescales the entire movement in time (25%–200% of its original duration) while keeping the same noise realisation — the noise is pre-generated for the longest (200%) duration, and only the portion matching the chosen duration is used. The noise variance slider scales the noise amplitude without changing its pattern.

What to observe

Noise introduces spurious high-frequency content into the speed profile. Because jerk is the second derivative of speed, even low-amplitude noise can dramatically inflate the integrated squared jerk, causing LDLJ to shift away from its true value.

Changing the duration rescales the time axis: a shorter (faster) movement has a higher peak speed relative to the same noise level — a better signal-to-noise ratio — so LDLJ is less affected. A longer (slower) movement has lower peak speed and is therefore more susceptible. Observe how the % change in LDLJ varies as you move the duration slider.

Enabling the low-pass filter removes high-frequency noise before computing LDLJ. A lower cut-off frequency attenuates more noise but can also distort the true movement signal — watch how the trade-off plays out. Heavy filtering can make a movement look smoother than it actually is by removing important movement components along with the noise.
