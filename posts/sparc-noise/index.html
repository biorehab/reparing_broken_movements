SPARC: Effect of Measurement Noise

Companion simulation for the blog post on the SPARC measure of movement smoothness

Real movement recordings always contain measurement noise. This simulation explores how additive Gaussian noise affects the SPARC smoothness estimate — and how a low-pass filter can mitigate that effect.

A random movement consisting of up to 8 minimum jerk trajectory (MJT) submovements is generated on demand. The duration slider rescales the entire movement in time (25%–200% of its original duration) while keeping the same noise realisation — noise is pre-generated for the longest (200%) duration, and only the portion matching the chosen duration is used. The noise variance slider scales the noise amplitude without changing its pattern.

What to observe

SPARC is inherently be robust to measurement noise, as there is no derivativ operations, and the cut-off frequency at 0.05 amplitude threshold of the normalised magnitude spectrum ensures high frequencies are excluded from the arc-length calculation. This gives SPARC a degree of built-in noise tolerance.
