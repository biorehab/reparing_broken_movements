LDLJ: Addition of Two MJT Submovements

Companion simulation for the blog post on the LDLJ measure of movement smoothness

A movement composed of two minimum jerk trajectory (MJT) submovements is less smooth than a single MJT — how much less smooth depends on how far apart the two submovements are in time. This simulation lets you explore that relationship for four jerk-based smoothness measures.

Both submovements have the same duration and unit amplitude. The inter-submovement interval (ISI) slider controls when the second submovement begins. The four lower charts trace each measure across the full range of ISI values — the vertical line and filled dot mark the currently selected ISI.

What to observe

Note that mean jerk measure does not have a monotonic response to the increasing ISI. In fact, the MJT is not the movement that has the least mean jerk measure!

Notice that DLJ, LDLJ, and SDLJ all have _nearly_ monotonic responses to ISI; there is small segment were the curve is non-monotonic. We will assume this to be negligible for practical purposes.
