LDLJ: Spatiotemporal Scaling Invariance

Companion simulation for the blog post on the LDLJ measure of movement smoothness

A reliable smoothness measure should not depend on how fast or how large a movement is — only on its shape. This simulation compares four jerk-based measures as the movement is scaled in amplitude ($\alpha$) and duration ($\beta$).

The random movement is a sum of up to five minimum jerk trajectory (MJT) submovements, each with velocity profile $v(t) = \frac{D_k}{T_k}\bigl[30\tau^2 - 60\tau^3 + 30\tau^4\bigr]$, $\tau = (t-t_k)/T_k$. Amplitude scaling multiplies the speed by $\alpha$; temporal scaling stretches the duration by $\beta$ and divides speed by $\beta$, preserving total displacement.

What do the measures show?

MJ (mean jerk / peak speed) is not fully dimensionless. Its invariant under amplitude scaling, but its not invariant under temporal scaling.

DLJ, LDLJ, and SDLJ are all derived from the dimensionless jerk $T^3 v_p^{-2}\!\int j^2\,dt$, which is invariant under both amplitude and temporal scaling. For amplitude scaling, jerk scales as $\alpha$ and peak speed $v_p$ also scales as $\alpha$, so $j^2$ and $v_p^2$ cancel. For temporal scaling, jerk scales as $1/\beta^3$ and the integration interval as $\beta$, giving $\int j^2\,dt \propto 1/\beta^5$; combined with $T^3 \propto \beta^3$ in the numerator and $v_p^2 \propto 1/\beta^2$ in the denominator, the net scaling is $\beta^3 \cdot \beta^2 \cdot \beta^{-5} = 1$. As a result, DLJ — and any monotone function of it such as LDLJ or SDLJ — remains unchanged regardless of how fast or how far the movement is performed.
