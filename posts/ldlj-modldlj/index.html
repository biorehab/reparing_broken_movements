LDLJ: Standard vs. Modified Measure

Companion simulation for the blog post on the LDLJ measure of movement smoothness

The standard LDLJ measure uses the squared 2-norm of jerk (the integrated squared jerk, $\int j^2 \, dt$) with normalisation $T^3 / v_p^2$: $$\text{LDLJ} = -\log\!\left(\frac{T^3}{v_p^2} \int j^2 \, dt\right)$$

The mod-LDLJ uses the L1-norm of jerk (integrated absolute jerk, $\int |j| \, dt$) with the correspondingly simpler normalisation $T / v_p$: $$\text{mod-LDLJ} = -\log\!\left(\frac{T}{v_p} \int |j| \, dt\right)$$ Both normalisations are dimensionless and invariant to amplitude and temporal scaling.

Each submovement has a 1-second duration and unit amplitude. The inter-submovement interval (ISI) is the onset-to-onset delay between consecutive submovements. Both measures are pre-computed across the full ISI range for 2 and 6 submovements. Drag the slider to inspect the speed profile and current measure values.

What to observe

Both measures are scale-invariant: at ISI = 0 all submovements overlap completely and the combined signal is simply a scaled copy of one submovement, so both LDLJ and mod-LDLJ equal their single-submovement values regardless of how many submovements are added.

As ISI increases the submovements separate and interact in increasingly complex ways. Observe whether the local non-monotonicities — the dips and bumps in the curves — appear similarly in both measures or whether one measure is more or less susceptible to them.

At ISI = 1 s the submovements are exactly end-to-end. At ISI > 1 s there are gaps between them; the normalisation grows faster than the jerk integral and both measures tend towards their single-submovement value once the submovements are sufficiently separated that they no longer interact.
