IMU Data During Planar Motion

What does an inertial measurement unit actually record during a straight arm movement?

1. IMU at Rest

What does a stationary IMU measure?

When at rest, the IMU is only sees acceleration due to gravity $\mathbf{g}$, through its local reference frame. When it's reference frame does not change orientation, the IMU outputs remain constant, equal to the components the gravity vector in its local reference frame. However, when the accelerometer is allowed to wiggle without underoing linear translation, the IMU measurements will wiggle around - the components of gravity are wiggling due to wiggling of its local reference frame.

$$ \mathbf{a}_{imu} = \mathbf{R}^\top \mathbf{g}$$ The derivative of the IMU signal $\mathbf{a}_{imu}$ is given by, $$ \frac{d\mathbf{a}_{imu}}{dt} = \left(\frac{d\mathbf{R}}{dt}\right)^\top \mathbf{g}$$ Note that for the same mount of orientation wiggling, faster wiggling with result in larger derivatives $\frac{d\mathbf{a}_{imu}}{dt}$. This issue has important implications for computing smoothness from the IMU signals using the dimensionless jerk approach.

2. IMU During Planar Motion

How do IMU readings change as the sensor moves through space?

Let's now see what happens when an IMU moves with the minimum jerk trajectory between two points. The acceleration signal in general will be, $$ \mathbf{a}_{imu} = \mathbf{R}^\top \left(\frac{d^2\mathbf{x}}{dt^2} + \mathbf{g}\right)$$ The derivative of the accelerometer data is given by, $$ \frac{d\mathbf{a}_{imu}}{dt} = \mathbf{R}^\top \frac{d^3\mathbf{x}}{dt^3} + \left( \frac{d\mathbf{R}}{dt}\right)^\top \frac{d^2\mathbf{x}}{dt^2} + \left(\frac{d\mathbf{R}}{dt}\right)^\top \mathbf{g}$$
