LDLJ-A: Addition of Two MJT Submovements

How does the acceleration-based smoothness measure respond to movement segmentation?

A movement composed of two minimum jerk trajectory (MJT) submovements is less smooth than a single MJT. The LDLJ-V measure quantifies this from the speed profile. The LDLJ-A measure does the same from the acceleration profile.

Both submovements have the same duration ($T_{sub} = 1\,\text{s}$) and unit amplitude. The inter-submovement interval (ISI) slider controls when the second submovement begins. The two lower charts trace LDLJ-V and LDLJ-A across the full ISI range — the vertical line and dot mark the currently selected value.

LDLJ-V uses the second derivative of speed as jerk, normalised by $v_\text{peak}^2 \cdot T^3$. LDLJ-A uses the first derivative of acceleration as jerk, normalised by $\bar{a}_\text{peak}^2 \cdot T$, where $\bar{a}$ is the mean-subtracted acceleration.
